In September 2023, when Google first let its AI assistant into Gmail, Docs and Drive, it made a clear promise about your content. This autumn it added a note to that promise, and then updated the Gemini privacy notice. If you use a personal Google account for anything that matters, it's worth knowing what the new wording says.

## tl;dr:

**What happened?** Google's 2023 promise that Gmail, Docs and Drive content would not be seen by human reviewers or used to train its AI now carries an update dated 16 October 2026. It says that with newer features turned on, that data can be used to train generative AI models and can be reviewed by humans in limited circumstances. The [Gemini Apps Privacy Notice](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961?hl=en-GB) was updated on 16 November 2026.

**What is the risk for UK users?** If you connect your email and files to Gemini and leave Gemini History on, Google says it uses data from those apps to train its models. Chats picked for human review are kept for up to three years, even after you delete them. And UK users get Gemini from Google LLC, the US company, not Google's Irish arm that serves Europe.

**What is the solution?** Check your settings today. For the files that really matter, keep them somewhere that has no AI reading them in the first place, on UK servers, under UK law.

## The 2023 promise

Google [launched its "Workspace extensions"](https://blog.google/products/bard/google-bard-new-features-update-sept-2023/) on 19 September 2023, back when the assistant was still called Bard. Letting an AI read your inbox was a big ask, so Google was reassuring:

> "If you choose to use the Workspace extensions, your content from Gmail, Docs and Drive is not seen by human reviewers, used by Bard to show you ads or used to train the Bard model."

That is a plain sentence, and plenty of people will have relied on it when they clicked "connect".

## The 2026 update

The same post now has a note in the middle of that paragraph:

> "(Update, October 16, 2026: ... As we've continued to make Google products more personalized and helpful, we've added features that enable users to choose to connect Workspace apps to other Google services. If users choose to enable these new features, data from Workspace Apps will be processed to personalize other Google experiences, and depending on a user's settings, that same subset of data can be used to train generative AI models. It can also be subject to human review in limited circumstances.)"

To be fair to Google, the note is careful. It talks about new features that users choose to turn on, and it says training depends on your settings. The original sentence is still there too. But read the two side by side. The first says your Gmail, Docs and Drive content is not seen by human reviewers or used for training. The second says it can be, under certain conditions. A promise that now depends on settings is a different kind of promise.

You'd also only see the change if you went back to a three-year-old blog post and read it again.

## What the new privacy notice says

The Gemini Apps Privacy Notice was updated on 16 November 2026. These are the parts worth reading.

**Connected apps can feed training.** In the section on the Gemini History setting, Google lists what it uses to train generative AI models when the setting is on. One of the items is "Data from apps that you connect to Gemini". It also says Gemini "saves and uses info from Connected Apps according to this notice, including to provide and improve Gemini Apps."

**Some chats are read by people.** "A subset of chats is reviewed by human reviewers (including Google's trained service providers) to help improve Google services." Google says those chats are disconnected from your account first.

**Deleting doesn't always delete.** "Chats reviewed by human reviewers ... are not deleted when you delete your history. Instead, they are retained for up to three years."

**Feedback is reviewed too, attachments included.** If you press thumbs up or thumbs down, your feedback and "any included content (like files or your personal content from connected apps)" are "Reviewed by specially trained teams."

**Google tells you not to share anything sensitive.** The notice says it plainly:

> "Please don't enter confidential information that you wouldn't want a reviewer to see or Google to use to improve our services, including machine-learning technologies."

That's sensible advice. It sits uneasily with a product whose main selling point is that it can read your email and files for you.

## Who you're actually dealing with

The privacy notice has one line about who provides the service:

> "Gemini Apps are provided by Google Ireland Limited in the European Economic Area and Switzerland, and by Google LLC everywhere else."

The UK isn't in the EEA, and it isn't Switzerland. So for UK users, the company processing your Gemini chats, and whatever you connect to them, is Google LLC in the United States. Europe gets an Irish company under EU rules. We are in "everywhere else".

That matters for the same reason we wrote about after [Anthropic cut off non-US users](/2026/07/anthropic-shutdown-wake-up-call-for-uk-data-sovereignty/) in June. When your data and the service around it belong to a US company, US decisions reach you, whether they come from Washington or from a privacy notice update.

## Does this affect my business?

It depends on your account. Google Workspace business and education accounts have [their own data terms](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gemini/generative-ai-in-google-workspace-privacy-hub), and Google says that content "is not human reviewed or otherwise used for Generative AI model training outside your domain without permission." That's a stronger commitment, and it's fair to say so.

But a lot of UK businesses don't run on Workspace. Sole traders, freelancers, therapists, small agencies and family firms often use a personal Gmail and a personal Drive, because it was free and it worked. Client contracts, invoices, ID scans, medical notes. If that describes you, the consumer privacy notice is the one that applies.

## What to do now

If you use Gemini on a personal account:

1. Open [Gemini Apps Activity](https://myactivity.google.com/product/gemini) and decide whether you want Gemini History on. With it off, Google says future chats won't be used for training unless you give feedback. They are still kept for around two months.
2. Check which apps are connected to Gemini, and disconnect any that hold things you wouldn't want a reviewer to see.
3. Think twice before pressing thumbs up or thumbs down on a chat that includes your files.

And look at where your important files actually live.
