faqs:
  - question: "Is Dropbox bad for privacy?"
    answer: "Bad is the wrong word. Dropbox encrypts data and has a reasonable security record. The issue is structural: it is a US company that holds the encryption keys, can access content, and is building AI features that process what you store. If none of that troubles you, Dropbox is a fine product. If it does, you want a provider whose legal position and business model are different, which is what Bigby is."
  - question: "How do I move my files from Dropbox to Bigby?"
    answer: "Your Dropbox folder is already an ordinary folder on your computer, so the simplest route is to copy it across with the Bigby desktop client or upload it through the web interface. For larger moves, connect to Bigby over WebDAV and copy everything with a standard sync tool. No conversion needed; files transfer as they are."
  - question: "Why does Dropbox offer more storage for the same price?"
    answer: "Scale, mostly. Dropbox runs storage for hundreds of millions of users and prices accordingly. Bigby is a small UK operation running UK infrastructure, and the subscription is our only revenue. We would rather charge an honest price for a private service than chase Dropbox on cost per terabyte, because we cannot win that race and do not intend to run it."
  - question: "Can a small team use Bigby the way we use Dropbox?"
    answer: "Yes. Shared folders, per-user accounts and browser-based document editing cover the core of how small teams use Dropbox. The Group plan gives three or more users the full Office tier from £4.99 per user per month. What you give up is the long tail of third-party integrations; what you gain is client data held in the UK under UK law."
